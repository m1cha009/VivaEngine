# M12: First small game: Lane Runner

## What was built

The Sandbox is now a game. **Lane Runner**: drive the milk truck down an endless three-lane road,
dodge the crates and collect the Cesium logo boxes while the speed rises from 50 to 144 km/h. One
crash ends the run.

- **Controls:** Space starts (and after a crash, starts again). A/D or the left and right arrow
  keys change lanes. F1 shows the debug windows, Esc quits.
- **Score:** a point per meter driven, plus 50 per logo box. The best score of the session stays
  on screen.
- **`--demo`** shows the engine's demo scene from M10 and M11 instead (the Mac checklist uses it).
- **The game, in `sandbox/src/`:**
  - `TruckController`: drives the truck (lanes, speed, steering);
  - `TruckWheels`: turns the model's wheels at the truck's speed (the demo scene uses it too);
  - `FollowCamera`: the chase camera;
  - `Treadmill`: keeps the road and the grass under the truck, so they never end;
  - `LaneRunner`: the game's rules: places crates and logo boxes, checks collisions, keeps the score
    and draws the HUD;
  - `Road.h`: the road's layout, which the truck, the rules and the road texture share;
  - `Smoothing.h`: one easing formula, used by the truck and the camera;
  - `LaneRunnerScene.cpp`: puts it all together, with road and grass textures made in code.
- **Engine features the game needed**, and nothing more:
  - `Component::OnLateUpdate`, Unity's `LateUpdate`: runs once every component's `OnUpdate` has run;
  - `Camera::BackgroundColor`, the sky (Unity's `backgroundColor`), which the scene hands to the new
    `Renderer::SetClearColor`;
  - `Transform::LookAt(point)` and `Transform::GetRotation()`, Unity's `transform.LookAt` and
    `transform.rotation`;
  - `Scene::Contains(gameObject)`, to tell whether an object is still there;
  - `Primitives::Plane(width, length, repeats)`, now one function for squares and rectangles (the
    road is a long one);
  - ImGui's default font is now added explicitly, so a game can add fonts of its own.

## Concepts

### A game made of components

The game is written the way you'd write it in Unity: small components on GameObjects, plus one
component that holds the rules.

| Unity | Lane Runner |
|---|---|
| A `GameManager` MonoBehaviour with the rules | `LaneRunner`, on the "Lane Runner" GameObject |
| A car controller script | `TruckController`, on the truck |
| Cinemachine, or a follow script in `LateUpdate` | `FollowCamera`, on the camera |
| `Instantiate(cratePrefab)` while playing | `scene.CreateGameObject` + `AddComponent<MeshRenderer>(sharedMesh, sharedMaterial)`, or `model->Instantiate` |
| `Destroy(gameObject)` | `scene.Destroy(gameObject)` |
| `Random.Range` | `std::mt19937` with a distribution (`<random>`) |
| UI Text, or `OnGUI` | Dear ImGui's draw lists |

`LaneRunner` is a **state machine**: the game is `Ready`, `Driving` or `Crashed`, and a `switch`
decides what each frame does. Space moves it from `Ready` to `Driving`; hitting a crate moves it
to `Crashed`; Space then restarts it.

### LateUpdate: following what moved

The truck moves in its `OnUpdate`, and the camera, the road and the rules need to see where it is
*now*, not where it was last frame. Within one pass the scene updates GameObjects in the order they
were created, but relying on that breaks as soon as something is created in another order, or
moves the truck from elsewhere. (A first version did rely on it, and the review caught the bug:
when a restart put the truck back at the start, the road had already followed the crash spot, and
for one frame the truck stood on the grass.)

Unity's answer is `LateUpdate`, and the engine now has it too: `Component::OnLateUpdate` runs in a
second pass over the scene, once every component's `OnUpdate` has run. So each frame goes:

```
OnUpdate:      TruckController moves the truck, LaneRunner starts a run on Space
OnLateUpdate:  FollowCamera and the Treadmills follow the truck, LaneRunner checks collisions
```

Whatever moves things goes in `OnUpdate`; whatever follows them, or checks where they ended up,
goes in `OnLateUpdate`. The order within each pass then doesn't matter.

### An endless road: the treadmill

The road is a 320 m strip, and the truck drives on for kilometers. The trick: whenever the truck
has gone another 8 m, the road jumps 8 m forward. Its texture repeats every 8 m (a 4 m dash and a
4 m gap), so after a jump every dash lies exactly where a dash was, and nobody can see that it
moved. That's `Treadmill`: it puts its object at `floor(truck z / period) × period`. The grass does
the same with its own 4 m texture.

The other common way is to keep adding road tiles ahead and removing them behind, like the crates.
For a road that looks the same everywhere, the treadmill is simpler.

### Spawning and removing

`LaneRunner` places a row every 28 m, once the truck is within 160 m of it. That far away a crate
is a few pixels tall, so its appearing goes unnoticed. In each row, one lane (or one time in three,
two) gets a crate, and each open lane gets a logo box one time in three. Items 15 m behind the
truck are out of the camera's view, and get destroyed.

Making these on the fly costs almost nothing, because nothing new goes to the GPU:

- **Crates** are GameObjects with a `MeshRenderer` that shares one cube mesh and one crate material.
  Creating a mesh or a texture would wait for the GPU (M8), which is why they're made once, in
  `LoadLaneRunnerScene`.
- **Logo boxes** are instances of the `BoxTextured` model (M11): `Instantiate` makes GameObjects
  that point at the model's mesh and texture.

Press F1 while driving and watch the Scene window: crates and boxes appear at the bottom of the
hierarchy and disappear from the top. A 30-second test at full speed kept the count at about 26
GameObjects and the GPU allocations at 25, so nothing piles up.

`LaneRunner` keeps plain pointers to its crates and boxes, and someone else can destroy them: the
inspector's Destroy button. A destroyed object is freed at the end of its frame, and using the
pointer after that would read freed memory. So before touching an item, the game asks
`scene.Contains(item)`, which only compares pointers, and forgets the item if it's gone: the
explicit version of Unity's `== null` check on a destroyed object. (The debug windows do the same
with their selection.)

### Collisions: boxes seen from above

There's no physics engine, and the game doesn't need one. Seen from above, the truck and every item
are rectangles lined up with the road. Two such rectangles overlap only if they overlap both across
the road (x) and along it (z):

```
|a.x - b.x| < halfWidthA + halfWidthB   and   |a.z - b.z| < halfLengthA + halfLengthB
```

That's an **axis-aligned bounding box** (AABB) test, the simplest collision test there is, and what
a physics engine does first too, before any finer test. The boxes are a little smaller than the
models, so a near miss stays a miss. Hitting a crate ends the run; touching a logo box collects it.

### Smooth movement without physics

Two small formulas do all the smoothing, and both give the same result at any frame rate:

- **Moving towards a target at a steady speed** (Unity's `Mathf.MoveTowards`): the truck's
  sideways step is `kSideSpeed × dt`, clamped to the distance left, so it stops exactly on the lane.
- **Easing towards a target** ("exponential smoothing"): `value += (target - value) × (1 - e^(-k × dt))`.
  Each frame closes a share of the gap, and the share depends on `dt` so that a second of 60 frames
  and a second of 144 frames close the same amount. The common `value = Lerp(value, target, 0.1f)`
  without `dt` eases faster on a faster PC. `SmoothTowards` in `Smoothing.h` does it, for the
  truck's nose and the camera's sideways follow.

The truck's nose turns into a lane change by up to 8°, in proportion to how fast it's sliding. Its
wheels roll at its speed, `speed / radius` as in M11, through `TruckWheels`: the one place that
knows the model's axles, their direction and the wheel size, now shared with the demo scene. The
speed itself rises by 0.4 m/s every second, from 14 m/s (50 km/h) to 40 m/s (144 km/h).

The road's layout lives in `Road.h`: the number of lanes, their width, and where each lane's
center is. The truck, the rules (which lanes get crates) and the road texture (where the lines go)
all read it, so changing `kLaneCount` to 4 gives a four-lane road that still works.

### The chase camera and LookAt

`FollowCamera` stays 6 m above and 10 m behind the truck, and looks at a point 14 m ahead of it.
Height and distance follow exactly, since a camera lagging behind a truck at 40 m/s would fall far
back. Sideways, it eases after the truck, so a lane change swings the view gently.

It aims with the new `Transform::LookAt(point)`, Unity's `transform.LookAt`. LookAt builds the
rotation from the three axes it should have:

- **+Z** (Forward) points at the target;
- **+X** is at right angles to both up and +Z, which is what the **cross product** of two
  directions gives;
- **+Y** is at right angles to the other two (another cross product).

The three axes are the columns of a rotation matrix, and `glm::quat_cast` turns that into a
quaternion. For an object with a parent, the parent's rotation (the new `GetRotation()`) is taken
back out with its inverse, because `LocalRotation` is relative to the parent.

### Textures made in code

The road and the grass have no image files: `LaneRunnerScene.cpp` fills arrays of pixels and hands
them to `Renderer::CreateTexture` (M11), the way Unity's `Texture2D.SetPixels32` works. The bytes
are sRGB colors, like a PNG's.

- **The road** is 256 × 64 pixels for 12.5 m × 8 m of road. White pixels where the lines are,
  measured in meters from the middle: solid lines at the edges, and dashes between the lanes in
  the top half only. The rest is gray asphalt with a little random noise.
- **The grass** is 64 × 64 pixels of green noise.

The engine makes mipmaps for them like for any texture (M7). That's what keeps the thin lane lines
from flickering in the distance.

### A HUD with Dear ImGui

The score isn't in a window. `LaneRunner` writes it straight onto ImGui's **background draw list**:
a layer drawn over the 3D scene but under ImGui's windows, so the debug windows (F1) cover the HUD
instead of the other way round. Each text is drawn twice: a dark copy a little offset as a shadow,
then the white text, which reads well on both the sky and the road.

Big text needs a font that scales. ImGui's default font is a pixel font, sharp at its own size
only, so `LoadLaneRunnerScene` adds ImGui's built-in **scalable** version of it
(`AddFontDefaultVector`) for the HUD. ImGui treats the first font added as the default, so the
engine now adds the default font itself when it creates the ImGui context (`Window.cpp`). The debug
UI keeps its pixel font, whatever fonts a game adds later.

### Depth precision: the near plane

The grass lies 5 cm under the road. Far away, the depth buffer can't tell 5 cm apart, and the two
surfaces would flicker through each other ("z-fighting"). Depth precision is spread very unevenly:
most of it goes to the first meters in front of the camera, and how much depends mostly on the near
plane. The smallest difference the depth buffer resolves at distance *z* is roughly
*z²/near* × 0.00000006:

| near plane | at 100 m | at 290 m (the end of the road) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 m (the default) | 6 mm | 5 cm: flickers |
| 0.5 m (the game's camera) | 1.2 mm | 1 cm: fine |

Nothing ever gets closer than half a meter to the game's camera, so it sets `NearPlane = 0.5`. Rule
of thumb, in Unity too: make the near plane as far as the game allows.

### The sky

`Camera::BackgroundColor` is the color the frame starts from (Unity's `backgroundColor` with Clear
Flags set to Solid Color). Each frame, `Scene::Render` hands the main camera's color to the
renderer, which clears the image to it. Like every color the engine draws with, it's linear: the
sRGB swapchain shows `(0.30, 0.55, 0.87)` as a light blue of about (150, 195, 240).

## Reading guide

1. **`sandbox/src/LaneRunnerScene.cpp`**: how the game is put together, and why in that order. The
   road and grass textures.
2. **`sandbox/src/TruckController.h/.cpp`**: input, the lanes, MoveTowards and smoothing, the
   wheels.
3. **`sandbox/src/LaneRunner.h/.cpp`**: the state machine, spawning, collisions, the score and the
   HUD.
4. **`sandbox/src/FollowCamera.cpp`** and **`sandbox/src/Treadmill.cpp`**: short, and each one
   trick.
5. **`sandbox/src/Road.h`**, **`sandbox/src/Smoothing.h`** and **`sandbox/src/TruckWheels.cpp`**:
   the shared pieces.
6. The engine's side:
   - **`engine/include/Viva/Component.h`** and `Scene::Update` in **`engine/src/Scene/Scene.cpp`**:
     `OnLateUpdate`, and `Scene::Contains` next to it;
   - **`engine/src/Scene/Transform.cpp`**: `GetRotation` and `LookAt`;
   - **`engine/include/Viva/Camera.h`**, `Scene::Render` in **`engine/src/Scene/Scene.cpp`** and
     `SetClearColor` in **`engine/src/Renderer/Renderer.cpp`**: the sky;
   - **`engine/src/Core/Primitives.cpp`**: the rectangle;
   - **`engine/src/Platform/Window.cpp`**: the default font.
7. **`sandbox/src/Main.cpp`**: `--demo`, and which scene starts.

## How to test

1. **Run** the Sandbox. The window title says "VivaEngine Lane Runner", and the HUD says
   "Press Space to drive". The debug windows are hidden (F1).
2. **Drive:** press Space. The truck sets off at 50 km/h and the speed keeps rising; the score
   counts the meters.
3. **Change lanes** with A and D, or the arrow keys. The truck slides over in about a third of a
   second, its nose turning into the move, and the camera swings after it.
4. **Collect** a logo box: 50 points, and the counter goes up.
5. **Crash** into a crate: "Crashed!", the truck stops, and Best keeps your best score. Space
   starts again.
6. **F1** while driving: watch the crates and boxes come and go in the Scene window. The number of
   GameObjects stays around 30, and the Stats window's GPU allocations stay at 25. Select a crate
   ahead and press Destroy: it's gone, and the game carries on without it.
7. **`Sandbox --demo`** (or `--demo` in CLion's run configuration): the M11 demo scene, unchanged.
8. **Resize** the window and **minimize** it: the HUD stays in its corners, and the game pauses
   while minimized.
9. **Experiments:**
   - Tune the game: `StartSpeed`, `MaxSpeed` and `Acceleration` in `TruckController.h`, the row
     spacing and pickup chance in `LaneRunner.cpp`.
   - Make it harder: block two lanes in every row, or set `kLaneCount` to 4 in `Road.h`.
   - A driver's view: set the camera's `Offset` to `{ 0, 2, 1 }` and its `LookAhead` to
     `{ 0, 1.8, 20 }`. You see out through the cab, because the truck's walls face away from you
     there, and back faces are culled.
   - Change `kSkyColor` to a sunset.
   - Add roadside posts with a third `Treadmill`: a parent object that steps by their spacing,
     with posts as its children.

## Known limitations

- **No physics:** collisions are box tests, and a crash just stops the truck.
- **No sound.** SDL3 has audio; it's on the list for after M12.
- **The best score isn't saved** between runs of the program, and there's no pause (minimizing
  pauses).
- **Destroying the truck or the camera** from the debug windows breaks the game: the other
  components keep pointers to them without checking. (Crates and logo boxes are checked.)
- **The look is flat:** no lighting, no shadows, no fog. Rows appear 160 m ahead, small enough not
  to be noticed.
- **The debug windows cover the HUD** while they're shown.
