# M6: 3D: transforms, depth, camera

## What was built

The engine draws a 3D scene that you can fly around in: a checkerboard floor, a ring of eight
pillars, and a cube spinning above the middle.

- **`Viva::Camera`** (`engine/include/Viva/Camera.h`, public): a position, a yaw and pitch, a field
  of view and clip planes. It turns them into its **world matrix**, a **view** matrix and a
  **projection** matrix. `Application::GetCamera()` gives the game the camera to move, and the
  renderer draws from it.
- **`FlyCamera`** (`sandbox/src/FlyCamera.h/.cpp`): Scene-view-style controls in the sandbox.
  - The new **`Input::SetCursorLocked()`** hides and locks the cursor while the right mouse button
    is held.
  - The sandbox no longer logs every key press (that was M1's input demo).
- **Push constants:** each draw call gets its object's **model matrix**.
- **`FrameUniforms`** (`engine/src/Renderer/FrameUniforms.h/.cpp`): one **uniform buffer** per
  frame in flight holds the camera's view and projection. The shaders reach it through a
  **descriptor set** (layout, pool and sets).
- **Depth buffer:**
  - a new **`Image`** class (`engine/src/Renderer/Image.h/.cpp`: a VMA image plus its view) holds
    a `D32_SFLOAT` depth image;
  - it's rebuilt with the swapchain;
  - the pipeline tests depth.
- **Back-face culling**, with counter-clockwise front faces.
- **`PipelineSettings`** gained `DescriptorSetLayouts`, `PushConstantRanges`, `DepthFormat` and
  `CullMode`.
- **`VulkanHelpers`** (`engine/src/Renderer/VulkanHelpers.h/.cpp`): the small helpers several
  classes share. That's command pool, command buffer, fence and semaphore creation, plus
  `TransitionImage`, which now takes a struct with named fields and works for depth images too.

## Concepts

### From model to screen: three matrices

A mesh is built around its own origin (**model space**): the cube runs from −0.5 to 0.5. Three 4×4
matrices take each vertex to the screen. The vertex shader applies them right to left:

```glsl
gl_Position = camera.Projection * camera.View * object.Model * vec4(inPosition, 1.0);
```

1. **Model** (per object): model space → **world space**. It's the object's Transform:
   `translate * rotate * scale`, so the mesh is scaled, then rotated, then moved. The eight pillars
   and the spinning cube all draw the *same* cube mesh with different model matrices.
2. **View** (per frame): world space → **view space**, where the camera sits at the origin looking
   down −Z. It's the **inverse** of the camera's own transform (`Camera::WorldMatrix`, built like
   any model matrix): moving the camera right is the same as moving the world left. Unity calls it
   `worldToCameraMatrix`.
3. **Projection** (per frame): view space → **clip space**, adding perspective (far things get
   smaller). `glm::perspective` builds it from the vertical field of view, the aspect ratio and
   the near and far planes. That's Unity's Camera "Field of View" and "Clipping Planes".

Unity does exactly this with `unity_ObjectToWorld`, `UNITY_MATRIX_V` and `UNITY_MATRIX_P`, built
for you from Transforms and the Camera.

### Which way is up: coordinate conventions

The engine is **right-handed with Y up**: +X right, +Y up, and the camera looks down **−Z**. That's
glTF's convention (M11's models), and Blender's and OpenGL's. **Unity is left-handed**: it looks
down +Z. So a scene from Unity would come out mirrored unless you flip Z.

Triangles are **front-facing when their corners appear counter-clockwise** on screen, also as in
glTF. Unity uses clockwise. `Cube()` and `Floor()` in `Renderer.cpp` build every triangle
counter-clockwise as seen from outside.

### Vulkan's clip space: Y points down, depth runs 0 to 1

After projection, the GPU divides by `w` (that division is what creates perspective) and lands in
**normalized device coordinates**:

- **x** from −1 (left) to +1 (right).
- **y** from −1 to +1, with **−1 at the top** in Vulkan. OpenGL has −1 at the bottom.
  - `Camera::ProjectionMatrix` returns an OpenGL-style matrix (GLM's `perspective`), as Unity's
    `Camera.projectionMatrix` does.
  - The renderer flips its y scale (`projection[1][1] *= -1`, in `DrawFrame`) when it hands the
    matrix to the GPU, which keeps +Y pointing up on screen. That's Unity's
    `GL.GetGPUProjectionMatrix`.
  - So the Vulkan detail stays in the renderer, and the camera's matrices mean the same as
    everywhere else.
- **depth** from 0 (near plane) to 1 (far plane). OpenGL uses −1 to 1. The
  `GLM_FORCE_DEPTH_ZERO_TO_ONE` define (since M0, in `cmake/Dependencies.cmake`) makes GLM produce
  Vulkan's range.

Forget either one and the picture is upside down, or the depth test misbehaves near the camera.

### The depth buffer

Without depth, triangles simply cover whatever was drawn before them, so the last draw wins. The
**depth buffer** is an image the size of the screen that stores, for every pixel, how far away the
nearest thing drawn there so far is.
1. It's cleared to 1.0 (the far plane) at the start of the frame.
2. For each new pixel, the GPU compares its depth with the stored one
   (`VK_COMPARE_OP_LESS_OR_EQUAL`).
3. Only a pixel at least as near is drawn, and its depth replaces the stored value.

That's Unity's `ZTest LEqual` + `ZWrite On`, the default for opaque shaders.

- **Format:** `VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT`, one 32-bit float per pixel. `VulkanContext::CheckDevice`
  makes sure the GPU supports it.
- **Lifetime:** it must match the swapchain images' size, so `RecreateSwapchain` rebuilds it.
- **Store op:** its contents aren't needed after the frame, so its store op is `DONT_CARE`, which
  lets the GPU skip writing it back to memory.

### Back-face culling

A closed mesh like the cube only ever shows its outside. The triangles facing away from the camera
are always hidden behind the front ones, so the GPU can skip them entirely: **back-face culling**
(Unity's `Cull Back`). The GPU decides "away" by the winding: corners that appear clockwise on
screen mean you're looking at the back. Fly *into* a pillar and it vanishes, because from inside
every face is a back face.

### Push constants vs uniform buffers

Shaders get data from outside in two ways here:

- **Push constants** are a few bytes (at least 128 on every GPU) recorded straight into the
  command buffer with `vkCmdPushConstants`. They're ideal for small per-draw data, like a model
  matrix (64 bytes). Unity sends each object's `unity_ObjectToWorld` in a small per-draw constant
  buffer for the same reason.
- **Uniform buffers** are regular buffers the shader reads. Use them for data shared by many draws,
  like the camera. The C++ struct (`CameraUniforms`) must match the GLSL block's memory layout
  (the "std140" rules); two `mat4`s line up trivially.

### Descriptor sets

A shader can't take a buffer pointer. It names a **set** and a **binding**
(`layout(set = 0, binding = 0) uniform Camera`), and a **descriptor set** says which buffer sits
there. Three objects are involved (`FrameUniforms.cpp`):

1. **`VkDescriptorSetLayout`**: the shape ("binding 0 is a uniform buffer read by the vertex
   shader"). The pipeline layout is built from it, together with the push constant range.
2. **`VkDescriptorPool`**: memory that sets are allocated from.
3. **`VkDescriptorSet`**: an actual set, written once with `vkUpdateDescriptorSets` to point at a
   buffer, and bound before drawing with `vkCmdBindDescriptorSets`.

It's comparable to how a Unity material holds references to its textures and buffers, which Unity
binds when it draws. M7 adds a second set, for textures.

### One uniform buffer per frame in flight

With two frames in flight (M3), the GPU may still be drawing frame N while the CPU prepares frame
N+1. If both used one uniform buffer, the CPU would overwrite the camera the GPU is still reading.
So there's **one buffer and one descriptor set per frame in flight**, and the renderer writes a
frame's buffer only after waiting for that frame's fence (`DrawFrame`, step 2). The same rule will
apply to anything the CPU changes every frame.

The depth buffer is different: there's only one, shared by both frames. That works because the
GPU runs frames in order, and the barrier at the start of each frame waits for the previous
frame's depth tests (the `EARLY/LATE_FRAGMENT_TESTS` stages) before clearing it.

### Mouse look and a locked cursor

Turning with the mouse needs movement deltas, not positions, and the cursor must not stop at the
screen's edge. `FlyCamera` calls `Input::SetCursorLocked(true)` while the right button is held, the
equivalent of Unity's `Cursor.lockState = CursorLockMode.Locked`.
- Under the hood it's SDL's **relative mouse mode**: the cursor is hidden and frozen, and
  `Input::MouseDelta()` keeps reporting raw movement.
- SDL only applies it while the window has keyboard focus.

`FlyCamera` turns the mouse movement into yaw (around the world's up axis) and pitch (clamped to
±89°, so the camera can't flip over). It moves along the camera's forward and right vectors scaled
by `dt`, so the speed doesn't depend on the frame rate. Those vectors are columns of the camera's
world matrix: column 0 is its right, and column 2 its back.

## Reading guide

1. **`shaders/VertexColor.vert`**: the `Camera` uniform block (set 0, binding 0), the
   `push_constant` block, and the matrix chain in `main()`.
2. **`engine/include/Viva/Camera.h`** and **`engine/src/Core/Camera.cpp`**: `WorldMatrix()`
   (translate, then rotate twice), the axes read from its columns, and `ViewMatrix()` as its
   inverse.
3. **`sandbox/src/FlyCamera.cpp`** (the cursor lock, the look and the movement), then
   **`sandbox/src/Main.cpp`** (`OnStart` places the camera; `OnUpdate` runs the fly camera).
4. **`engine/src/Renderer/FrameUniforms.h`** and **`.cpp`**: `CameraUniforms`, then `Create()`'s
   four steps: layout, pool, sets, buffers.
5. **`engine/src/Renderer/Pipeline.h`**: the new settings. In **`Pipeline.cpp`**, look at the depth
   stencil state, `depthAttachmentFormat`, `cullMode`/`frontFace`, and the pipeline layout built
   from set layouts and push constant ranges.
6. **`engine/src/Renderer/Image.h`** and **`.cpp`**: image + view, allocated by VMA. M7's textures
   use it too.
7. **`engine/src/Renderer/Renderer.cpp`**:
   - `Cube()`, `Floor()` and the two transform functions at the top.
   - `Create()` builds the pipeline with all the new settings.
   - `RecreateSwapchain()` builds the depth image.
   - `DrawFrame()`: step 2 writes the uniforms (with the y flip), then come the depth barrier
     and the depth attachment.
   - `RecordDraws()` binds the descriptor set once and pushes a model matrix per draw.
8. **`engine/src/Renderer/VulkanHelpers.h`**: `ImageTransition`, then the creation helpers that
   `FrameResources` and `ImmediateSubmit` now share.

## How to test

1. **Run** the Sandbox. You start behind the pillar ring, looking slightly down at the spinning
   cube.
2. **Fly:** W/A/S/D move, Q/E go down/up, and holding Shift is three times faster.
3. **Look:** hold the right mouse button and move the mouse. The cursor disappears while you hold
   it, and you can turn all the way around.
4. **Depth:** fly so a pillar passes in front of the cube, and it hides the cube correctly.
5. **Culling:** fly into a pillar: it disappears from the inside. Fly under the floor and look up:
   the floor is invisible from below.
6. **Resize** to a tall, narrow window: nothing stretches, because the aspect ratio follows the
   window.
7. **RenderDoc:** capture a frame.
   - The *Texture Viewer* shows the depth buffer (near is dark, far is light).
   - On any draw, *Pipeline State* shows depth test LESS and cull BACK, plus the push constant
     range and the descriptor set with the uniform buffer.
   - The *Event Browser* shows `vkCmdPushConstants` before each draw.
8. **Experiments** (rebuild after each, and revert afterwards):
   - In `Pipeline.cpp`, set `.depthTestEnable = VK_FALSE`. Objects are now drawn in order: the
     spinning cube (drawn last) shows through pillars standing in front of it.
   - Set `.CullMode = VK_CULL_MODE_FRONT_BIT` in `Renderer::Create`. You see the cube's inner faces,
     as if it were inside out.
   - Remove `projection[1][1] *= -1.0f` in `Renderer::DrawFrame`. The world turns upside down.
     Because that also reverses the winding on screen, culling now removes the faces you'd see.
   - In `Main.cpp`'s `OnStart`, set `camera.FieldOfView = glm::radians(100.0f)`, or 30.

## Known limitations

- **Mouse look wasn't scripted.** The test harness posts window messages, and SDL reads the real
  mouse button's state, so it releases a posted right-drag at once. Movement, depth, culling and
  resizing were tested by script; mouse look by its math only. Please try it.
- **WASD always works.** In Unity's Scene view it only works while the right button is held.
- **No lighting yet:** faces get their color from the vertices.
- **Depth precision:** standard depth (near = 0, far = 1) loses precision far away. "Reversed Z"
  is the usual fix, and it doesn't matter at this scale.
- **Tile-based GPUs** (Apple) could keep this depth buffer entirely on-chip, using
  `TRANSIENT_ATTACHMENT` usage and the LAZILY_ALLOCATED memory the Mac reports. We don't, yet.
- **The scene and its single pipeline live in the renderer.** M8 moves "what to draw" into the
  game.

  (clang-tidy) are clean.
